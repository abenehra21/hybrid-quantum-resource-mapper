# Phase 4 results: automatic partitioning

Generated by `python -m hybrid_mapper report` from `data/plans.csv`: 3288 candidate partitions of
1920 applications, each executed in the Phase 3 ground-truth model
(`python -m hybrid_mapper plans`). The applications are the 2432 Phase 3
tasks plus 384 tasks on *clustered* circuits (fully entangled blocks
joined by a few CZ gates), 2816 in all. Sampling tasks and fully entangled circuits have no
candidate: there is nothing to cut.

A partition's parts go back through Phase 2 and Phase 3 as ordinary tasks. A Max-Cut part must be solved
exactly; a circuit fragment needs precision eps / 3^cuts and runs
6^(cuts touching it) circuit variants. The plan is then scheduled as a task DAG on
4 classical nodes and one QPU.

## Two checks that do not depend on the ground-truth model

**Gate cutting gives the right answer.** `cut_expectation` rebuilds <H> of five small clustered circuits
(6-9 qubits, 1-2 cut gates) from their fragments alone. Largest difference from the uncut statevector
value: 9.8e-15. The same decomposition has 6 circuits per cut gate and weights of total
magnitude 3, which is where the 9x shots per cut in the cost model comes from.

**Max-Cut plans are really solved.** All 59 distinct Max-Cut partitions were executed (every part
by enumeration, then the merge). Each returns a cut and a proof of its quality: certified ratio =
cut / (sum of part optima + crossing edges), which can only understate the true ratio. Certified ratios:
minimum 0.886, median 0.939; the classical
heuristic the monolithic task can use guarantees 0.878.

| vqe | REDESIGN | REDESIGN | 32 |

**351 of the 749 tasks that are REDESIGN as one task have a working partition
(47%).** No task that already works gets worse, and chains (linear and circular
ansatz) are never partitioned: they are already easy for an MPS simulator.

**Parts sent to the QPU: 0.** Every part of every working plan is classical. In this model the
QPU's share of the work is sampling, which cannot be partitioned; cutting turns oversized optimisation and
modular chemistry tasks into classical work. A fragment is only worth a QPU when it is too wide and too
entangled to simulate yet shallow enough to survive noise, and no such fragment occurs in these families.

Planners: *no partitioning* is Phase 3 alone. *Partition whenever stuck* takes the first candidate
when Phase 3 says REDESIGN. The *rule planner* also requires every part to be allocatable by Phase 3 and at
most 2 cuts per fragment. The *learned planner* is a gradient-boosting classifier
trained on executed plans ("does this candidate work and beat the task as it is?"); it takes the accepted
candidate with the fewest cuts. Scoring one candidate takes 1.0 ms (Phase 2 predictions and Phase 3
decisions for every part, no transpile).

In the extrapolation split every test application is larger than anything in training, so the range guard
hands both learned components over to their rule versions: the learned planner equals the rule planner there.
